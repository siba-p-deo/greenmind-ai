# Smartphones

## Environmental Impact

At a category level, smartphones carry a high environmental footprint concentrated in manufacturing. Rare earth mineral extraction for components such as lithium, cobalt, and tantalum involves energy-intensive mining with documented ecosystem impacts. Semiconductor fabrication requires significant water and chemical inputs. Studies at the category level consistently show that the majority of a smartphone's lifetime carbon footprint — often cited as more than 70% — is locked in before the device is ever switched on. Frequent upgrade cycles amplify this impact across populations.

## Typical Lifespan

The average smartphone is replaced every two to three years, though devices are technically capable of functioning for five or more years. Software support cycles from manufacturers typically end between three and five years post-release, which often drives premature replacement.

## Repairability

Repairability in this category is generally low to moderate. Many devices use adhesive bonding and proprietary components that complicate battery and screen replacement. Independent repair is possible but varies significantly by model generation.

## Common Purchase Triggers

Upgrade cycles driven by carrier promotions, social comparison, cracked screens, declining battery life, and new feature announcements are the primary drivers at this category level.

## Sustainable Alternatives

Purchasing certified refurbished devices, extending use through battery replacement, and exploring secondhand markets are the most impactful alternatives.

## End-of-Life Considerations

Smartphones contain hazardous materials including lead and lithium. Certified e-waste recyclers can recover valuable materials. Landfill disposal is harmful and often illegal.

## GreenMind Guidance

This information is category-level and not specific to any product. The most impactful action in this category is extending the lifespan of an existing functional device rather than purchasing new.
