# Fast Fashion

## Environmental Impact

Fast fashion is among the most resource-intensive consumer categories at a global scale. Textile production is a major industrial consumer of water, and dyeing and finishing processes are associated with chemical discharge into waterways. Synthetic fabrics — polyester, nylon, acrylic — are derived from fossil fuels and shed microplastic particles during washing, which enter water systems and have been detected throughout marine and freshwater environments. At the category level, the fashion industry contributes meaningfully to global greenhouse gas emissions through production, transport, and textile waste.

## Typical Lifespan

Fast fashion garments are often designed for short use cycles, with average wear frequency declining significantly over the past two decades at a category level. Many items are discarded after fewer than ten wears.

## Repairability

Basic garment repair — seam mending, button replacement, zipper repair — is accessible and low-skill. Fast fashion construction quality, including thin fabrics and low-grade stitching, limits the return on repair effort relative to higher-quality garments.

## Common Purchase Triggers

Trend cycles, social media influence, seasonal sales, and social comparison are the dominant purchase drivers in this category.

## Sustainable Alternatives

Secondhand and vintage clothing markets, clothing swaps, garment rental for occasion wear, and choosing natural or certified-recycled fabrics in fewer, higher-quality pieces represent the most sustainable alternatives.

## End-of-Life Considerations

Textile recycling infrastructure is limited globally. Blended synthetic-natural fabrics are particularly difficult to recycle. Donation extends garment life but is not a solution to overproduction.

## GreenMind Guidance

This is category-level information. Buying less and wearing longer is consistently the highest-impact action in this category, ahead of material choice or recycling.
