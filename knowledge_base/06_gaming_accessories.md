# Gaming Accessories

## Environmental Impact

Gaming accessories — controllers, mice, keyboards, headsets, and peripheral devices — share the general electronics manufacturing footprint: printed circuit boards, plastic housings, copper wiring, and increasingly lithium batteries in wireless variants. At the category level, the volume of peripheral devices sold globally is substantial, and the culture of frequent upgrades driven by performance specifications and aesthetic trends amplifies cumulative environmental impact. RGB lighting features, while low individual power draw, contribute to a product design culture that prioritises novelty over longevity.

## Typical Lifespan

Mechanical keyboards and quality wired peripherals are among the most durable items in consumer electronics, with potential lifespans exceeding ten years. Controllers and wireless devices have shorter effective lifespans due to battery degradation, thumbstick drift, and trigger wear, typically three to six years.

## Repairability

This category has above-average repairability potential. Mechanical keyboard switches are individually replaceable. Controller thumbstick modules are a common and accessible repair. Many peripherals use standard screws and documented teardown procedures. A growing community of repair guides and aftermarket parts supports this category.

## Common Purchase Triggers

New console or platform adoption, component failure, performance upgrade desire, aesthetic trends, and competitive gaming specifications drive purchases in this category.

## Sustainable Alternatives

Repairing thumbstick drift and worn switches rather than replacing devices, purchasing secondhand controllers and peripherals, and choosing wired peripherals with documented repairability are the most sustainable approaches.

## End-of-Life Considerations

Gaming accessories contain electronic components and, in wireless variants, lithium batteries. E-waste facilities can process these materials; landfill disposal is not appropriate.

## GreenMind Guidance

This information is category-level. Controller stick drift and keyboard switch failure are both commonly repairable, making repair the recommended first response to malfunction in this category.
